Auditor prep

Documents to have on hand

Auditors call this the "PBC" (prepared-by-client) list. Gathering these before fieldwork starts is the single biggest lever for keeping your audit or AUP engagement on schedule and under budget — missing records are the most common cause of delays.

Core financials

General Ledger & Trial Balance

  • Year-end trial balance for every fund
  • General ledger detail and chart of accounts
  • All adjusting and closing journal entries
  • Interfund transfer and due to/due from schedules
🔒Store in: your accounting/ERP system, with role-based access and automatic encrypted backup
Core financials

Bank & Investment Records

  • Bank statements and reconciliations for every account through year-end
  • Investment and CD/pooled-investment statements
  • Collateralization documentation for public deposits
  • Outstanding check lists and voided-check logs
🔒Store in: your bank's secure portal plus an encrypted financial-records archive, access restricted to finance staff
Core financials

Budget Documents

  • Adopted original budget and all amendments
  • Budget-to-actual reports for every fund
  • Governing body minutes approving the budget and amendments
🔒Store in: your public records / document management system, with a version history of every amendment
Governance

Governance & Legal

  • Minutes of all governing body meetings for the fiscal year
  • Charter, bylaws, or enabling legislation
  • Insurance policies (property, liability, public officials, fidelity/crime bond)
  • Attorney representation letter and litigation/claims summary
  • Major contracts and intergovernmental agreements
🔒Store in: a fireproof records vault for signed originals, with encrypted digital copies in your document management system
Assets & debt

Capital Assets & Long-Term Debt

  • Capital asset ledger (additions, disposals, depreciation)
  • Construction-in-progress detail
  • Lease agreements (GASB 87)
  • Debt schedules, amortization tables, and debt service to maturity
  • Bond covenants, official statements, and continuing disclosure filings
🔒Store in: your capital asset management system, with deed/title originals in a locked, access-logged records vault
Payroll

Payroll & Benefits

  • Payroll registers and year-end W-2s/1099s
  • Pension (GASB 68) and OPEB (GASB 75) reports from your plan administrator
  • Compensated absence / leave balance schedules
  • Employment contracts for key officials, if applicable
🔒Store in: your HR/payroll system with restricted, role-based access — this category contains SSNs and other PII
Revenue

Revenue Support

  • Property tax digest and collection detail
  • SPLOST collections and project-by-project expenditure ledger
  • Hotel/Motel tax collections and use-of-funds documentation
  • Franchise fee, license, and permit revenue detail
  • Utility billing and accounts receivable aging
🔒Store in: your tax/revenue collection system, with an encrypted archive and audit trail for collections detail
Federal funds

Federal Grants (if Single Audit applies)

  • Grant award letters and agreements for every federal program
  • Support for the Schedule of Expenditures of Federal Awards (SEFA)
  • Procurement records for grant-funded purchases
  • Subrecipient monitoring documentation, if funds were passed through
🔒Store in: a grants management system with document retention controls, mapped to each award's compliance requirements
Wrap-up

Payables & Prior-Year Items

  • Accounts payable aging and outstanding invoices at year-end
  • Accrued liability support (payroll, compensated absences, claims)
  • Prior-year audit report and management letter
  • Status/evidence of corrective action on prior-year findings
🔒Store in: your accounts payable system, with prior-year audit reports retained per your records retention schedule
🛡️

General storage & retention practices

Whatever system you use, records containing financial data or PII should sit behind access controls (unique logins, role-based permissions, an audit trail of who viewed or changed a file) and be backed up somewhere offsite or in the cloud, encrypted at rest and in transit. Keep signed originals of governance documents (minutes, bonds, contracts) in a fireproof, locked location. Retention periods are set by Georgia's local government records retention schedules — published by the Georgia Archives — and vary by record type, so confirm the applicable schedule before destroying anything.

Not sure what applies to your entity?

Not every category applies to every local government — a small city with no federal awards, for example, can skip the Single Audit documentation entirely. We can review your fund structure and hand you a PBC list scoped to exactly what your auditor will ask for.

A Single Audit is a federally required audit of compliance with the terms of federal grants, triggered when an entity expends $1,000,000 or more in federal awards in a fiscal year (2 CFR 200.501, as revised by OMB in 2024). It's performed alongside — not instead of — your regular O.C.G.A. § 36-81-7 audit.
